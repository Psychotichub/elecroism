To integrate an Air Circuit Breaker with a BMS, you’re no longer dealing with a simple breaker—you’re using it as a remotely operated, protected switching device with feedback and interlocks.

I’ll explain it in a structured way: modules → terminals → control logic → sequence of operation.

1. ACB modules used in BMS control

A BMS-ready ACB typically includes:

🔹 Motor charging mechanism
Charges the closing spring automatically
🔹 Closing coil (CC)
Electrically closes the breaker
🔹 Shunt trip (ST)
Electrically trips (opens) the breaker
🔹 Undervoltage release (UVR)
Trips breaker if control voltage is lost
🔹 Auxiliary contacts (AX)
Feedback signals (ON/OFF/TRIP)
🔹 Trip unit (electronic)
Protection (LSIG)
🔹 Communication module (optional)
Modbus / BACnet for BMS
2. Main control terminals (important)

Terminal naming varies by manufacturer, but functionally they are:

🔹 Closing coil terminals (CC)
Purpose: Close the breaker
Input: short pulse (e.g., 230V AC or 24V DC)

👉 When energized → breaker closes

🔹 Shunt trip terminals (ST)
Purpose: Open (trip) breaker
Input: voltage signal

👉 When energized → breaker opens immediately

🔹 Undervoltage release (UVR)
Must be continuously energized to allow closing

👉 If voltage drops:

Breaker trips automatically
Prevents unsafe reclosing
🔹 Motor terminals
Supply to charge spring (often automatic)
🔹 Auxiliary contacts (feedback)

Dry contacts to BMS:

52a → breaker CLOSED
52b → breaker OPEN
Trip contact → fault indication
3. Control wiring concept
Typical logic:
BMS DO (ON command)  → Closing Coil (CC)
BMS DO (OFF command) → Shunt Trip (ST)

ACB AUX contacts → BMS DI (status feedback)
4. Sequence of operation
🔹 Closing (BMS turns ON supply)
Spring is charged (motor)
UVR is energized (healthy condition)
BMS sends pulse to closing coil
Mechanism releases → contacts close
AUX contact (52a) → sends “ON” to BMS
🔹 Opening (BMS turns OFF supply)
BMS energizes shunt trip
Trip latch releases
Contacts open
AUX contact (52b) → sends “OFF” to BMS
🔹 Fault condition (automatic trip)
Overcurrent / fault detected by trip unit
ACB trips internally
Sends trip signal to BMS

👉 Important: this is independent of BMS

5. Interlocks (critical in real systems)

ACB will NOT close unless:

Spring is charged
UVR is energized
No trip condition
Mechanical interlocks satisfied
6. Typical control voltages
24V DC (most common in BMS)
110V DC (industrial)
230V AC (older systems)
7. Communication with BMS

Advanced ACBs include:

Modbus RTU / TCP
BACnet

BMS can read:

Current, voltage
Trip status
Energy data
8. Practical example (main incomer)

In a building:

ACB = main incomer breaker
BMS can:
Turn building power ON/OFF
Monitor faults
Log events
9. Engineer-level insight
Closing coil = controlled ON
Shunt trip = forced OFF
UVR = safety lock
Aux contacts = feedback loop

Design principle:

BMS sends commands, ACB enforces safety

10. Common mistakes
Trying to hold closing coil energized (wrong → it’s a pulse)
Not wiring UVR → breaker won’t close
No interlock → unsafe operation
No feedback → BMS blind control
Final takeaway

An ACB in BMS works as:

Remotely controlled switch (via CC & ST)
Protection device (via trip unit)
Feedback provider (via AUX contacts)

🔷 ACB + BMS CONTROL WIRING DIAGRAM
1. Control Power Supply
+24V DC  ───────────────┐
                        │
                       (FUSE)
                        │
                        ├──────────────→ UVR (Undervoltage Release)
                        │
                        ├──────────────→ Closing Coil (CC) via BMS relay
                        │
                        └──────────────→ Shunt Trip (ST) via BMS relay

0V (GND) ─────────────────────────────→ Common return
2. BMS OUTPUT CONTROL (DO – Digital Outputs)
🔹 CLOSE COMMAND
BMS DO (ON) ──→ Relay ──→ ACB Closing Coil (CC)

[24V] ──(Relay NO Contact)──→ CC Terminal A1
[0V ] ─────────────────────→ CC Terminal A2

👉 Pulse signal (0.5–1 sec) → breaker CLOSES

🔹 OPEN COMMAND
BMS DO (OFF) ──→ Relay ──→ ACB Shunt Trip (ST)

[24V] ──(Relay NO Contact)──→ ST Terminal A1
[0V ] ─────────────────────→ ST Terminal A2

👉 Energize → breaker TRIPS (opens)

3. UVR (Undervoltage Release – mandatory)
[24V] ─────────────→ UVR A1
[0V ] ─────────────→ UVR A2

⚠️ If UVR loses power:

ACB trips
Cannot close
4. AUXILIARY CONTACTS (Feedback to BMS)

From Air Circuit Breaker:

🔹 Breaker CLOSED (52a)
ACB 52a ─────────→ BMS DI (ON status)
🔹 Breaker OPEN (52b)
ACB 52b ─────────→ BMS DI (OFF status)
🔹 TRIP SIGNAL
Trip contact ───→ BMS DI (FAULT alarm)

6. SEQUENCE OF OPERATION
✅ CLOSE from BMS
UVR energized
Spring charged (motor auto)
BMS sends pulse → CC
ACB closes
52a = ON signal to BMS
❌ OPEN from BMS
BMS energizes ST
Breaker trips
52b = OFF signal
⚠️ FAULT
Trip unit detects fault
ACB trips internally
Trip contact → BMS alarm
7. IMPORTANT PRACTICAL NOTES
⚠️ Do NOT:
Hold CC energized → coil damage
Skip UVR → unsafe operation
Use direct BMS output → always use relay interface
✅ Always:
Use interposing relays between BMS and ACB
Use fuse protection on control circuits
Label terminals clearly
